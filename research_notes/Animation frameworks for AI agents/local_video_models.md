# Local open-weight video generation on a 16 GB GPU (RTX 5080, Windows 11), as of October 2026

Scope: what local generative video can and cannot do for an agent-driven pipeline of short narrated animations (20 s to ~7 min, 1080p, 24-30 fps). The current pipeline renders procedural Python shots and uses still Qwen-Image plates (see `local-animations/LESSONS-FROM-INITIAL-CONDITION.md`: "Only one model stack fits on the card at a time"). Research done 2026-10-08. Most sources are vendor pages, model cards and third-party blogs. I found **no measured RTX 5080 benchmark** for any video model, so all timings below come from other GPUs or are estimates. Items marked *(prior knowledge, not re-verified)* come from the researcher's background knowledge (cutoff mid-2026) and were not confirmed against a source in this session.

## Q1. Which models fit in 16 GB VRAM, at what resolution and clip length, and how long a clip takes

### Takeaway
Three current families are realistic on 16 GB, all with quantization or offloading: **LTX-2.x** (LTX-2.3 from March 2026, LTX-2.5 from August 2026; 22B, FP8/int8/NVFP4, with audio), **Wan 2.2** (14B MoE via FP8/GGUF plus block swap, or the 5B TI2V model natively), and **HunyuanVideo 1.5** (8.3B, 14 GB with offload). Expect about 4-5 s clips at 480p-720p per generation. With distill/"lightning" LoRAs a clip takes a few minutes, and 20-45 min without them. 1080p comes from a separate upscale pass, not from native generation on this card. Wan 2.2 (July 2025) is still the newest open Wan. Wan 2.5, 2.6 and 3.0 are API-only.

### Cited Findings
**Landscape / what is current**
- LTX-2.5 was released 11 Aug 2026 as open weights: a 22B "asymmetric dual-stream diffusion transformer" generating video and audio jointly, with a Gemma 4 12B text encoder, dev and distilled checkpoints, ComfyUI-ready int8 "convrot" and NVFP4 quantizations of the distilled transformer, and 2x latent spatial and 2x latent temporal upscalers — [ComfyUI Wiki news](https://comfyui-wiki.com/en/news/2026-08-11-ltx-2-5-open-weights-release); [HF Lightricks/LTX-2.5](https://huggingface.co/Lightricks/LTX-2.5)
- LTX-2.5 HF card: the distilled DiT uses a fixed 8-step schedule with CFG=1; the NVFP4 distilled build targets Blackwell hardware (RTX 50xx is Blackwell); FP8 is a runtime flag (`--quantization fp8-cast`) plus a CPU-offload option; width/height must be divisible by 32 and frames must satisfy `frames % 8 == 1` (examples use 121 frames at 24 fps); shown resolutions include 1920x1088 and 3840x2176 via `--spatial-upscalings 2`. The card states no VRAM figure — [HF Lightricks/LTX-2.5](https://huggingface.co/Lightricks/LTX-2.5)
- LTX-2.5 VRAM on 16 GB is contested: vendor-style summaries say a 16 GB minimum for quantized variants ([Runpod blog](https://www.runpod.io/blog/ltx-2-5-the-open-weights-world-model-built-for-speed-and-how-to-run-it-on-runpod)); an LTX ComfyUI guide says full bf16 needs 32 GB+ and 16/24 GB cards need a quantized variant ([LTX ComfyUI workflow guide](https://ltx.io/blog/comfyui-workflow-guide)); a hands-on YouTube test measured 47 GB at 2 megapixels on an 80 GB card, so 16 GB holds only at lower resolution/lighter settings ([YouTube: LTX-2.5 full setup and test](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sIOC9pzHzjs), via search summary)
- LTX-2.5 speed claims are vendor-run on datacenter hardware: a 10 s clip from an image takes "roughly 6-8 seconds on NVIDIA superchips" (distilled), and 6.8 s was measured on two GB200s. No RTX figures were published — [ComfyUI Wiki](https://comfyui-wiki.com/en/news/2026-08-11-ltx-2-5-open-weights-release); [Runpod](https://www.runpod.io/blog/ltx-2-5-the-open-weights-world-model-built-for-speed-and-how-to-run-it-on-runpod)
- LTX-2 was announced Oct 2025, open-sourced 6 Jan 2026, and LTX-2.3 followed on 8 Mar 2026. FP8 weights are about 12 GB and bf16 24 GB. On a 16 GB card the guide recommends FP8 at 720p with clips of about 4 s. NVIDIA's NVFP8 build is "about 30% smaller and up to 2x faster on RTX". The guide cites comparison testing that puts LTXV 2-3x faster than Wan 2.2/HunyuanVideo at comparable settings (third-party, no measured 16 GB timings) — [AI Video Sensei LTX-2 local setup, 2026-08-03](https://aivideosensei.com/guides/ltx-2-local-setup)

- The official LTX FAQ: the full dev model needs 80 GB+, and the distilled/fp8 variants support 32 GB GPUs — [ltx.io llm-info](https://ltx.io/llm-info) (via search summary; direct fetch failed)
- Wan 2.2 (released 28 Jul 2025, Apache 2.0) is the last open-weights Wan flagship. Wan 2.5 (Sep 2025 preview) and Wan 2.6 (Dec 2025) are API-only. Wan 3.0 (launched about Aug 24, 2026, 30 s clips with audio) has no HF checkpoint, GitHub repo or license text, and Alibaba lists "Open Source: No". One site (wan27.org) claims open 1.3B/14B Wan 3.0 weights, but it traces to a single LinkedIn post and is internally inconsistent — [Atlas Cloud: Is Wan 3.0 open source](https://www.atlascloud.ai/blog/tips/is-wan-3.0-open-source); [LocalAIMaster: Wan 2.7](https://localaimaster.com/blog/wan-2-7-open-source); [wan27.org](https://wan27.org/blog/wan-3-open-source) (via search summary)
- Alibaba still releases smaller open Wan-family models: Wan2.2-Animate, and Wan-Dancer-14B (Apache 2.0, July 2026) — search summary of [LocalAIMaster](https://localaimaster.com/blog/wan-2-7-open-source) and others; unverified on HF

- RTX 5080: 16 GB GDDR7, 256-bit bus, 960 GB/s, and about 33% faster than an RTX 4080 in games (not a video-gen benchmark) — [Notebookcheck RTX 5080](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Nvidia-GeForce-RTX-5080-Benchmarks-and-Specs.935679.0.html) (via search summary)
- HunyuanVideo 1.5: 8.3B DiT; 480p and 720p T2V and I2V, with a super-resolution stage to 1080p; default 121 frames (about 5 s at 24 fps). "Minimum GPU Memory: 14 GB (with model offloading enabled)". The step-distilled 480p I2V model (5 Dec 2025, 8 or 12 steps recommended, 4 possible) "can generate videos within 75 seconds" on an RTX 4090, with end-to-end time "reduced by 75%". The original 13B HunyuanVideo needs 60-80 GB at 720p FP16 — (search summary, [packet.ai](https://packet.ai/blog/video-generation-gpu-requirements-ltx-hunyuan-wan))

- CogVideoX-2B (Apache 2.0) runs on 16 GB GPUs and is capped at 720p, but it is older and lower quality. CogVideoX-5B gives 6 s clips with lower VRAM than larger models — [Thunder Compute](https://www.thundercompute.com/blog/best-open-source-ai-video-generation-models) (via search summary); [Hyperstack, updated 16 Sep 2026](https://www.hyperstack.cloud/blog/case-study/best-open-source-video-generation-models)
- Mochi 1 (Genmo): 10B, Apache 2.0, 480p (640x480), up to 5.4 s at 30 fps — [Hyperstack](https://www.hyperstack.cloud/blog/case-study/best-open-source-video-generation-models). Another source lists Mochi 1 and unquantized Wan 2.2 as needing 32-48 GB — (search summary, [LTX blog](https://ltx.io/blog/best-open-source-video-generation-models))
- Newer open options (mostly aggregator claims): **Kandinsky 5** (2B Lite and 19B Pro, MIT; the developers claim 2B Lite beats Wan 5B/14B) — [GitHub kandinskylab/kandinsky-5](https://github.com/kandinskylab/kandinsky-5/); **LongCat-Video** (Meituan, 13.6B, MIT, T2V/I2V/video continuation, minute-scale 720p/30 fps, thin ecosystem); **SkyReels V3** (1-4 reference images, V2V, audio-guided, licence unconfirmed); **MAGI-2** (Sand AI, Apache 2.0, Aug 2026 preview; reported size conflicts: 114B MoE with about 6B active vs 24B, plus 4.5B/8B siblings) — [Cinevva reference guide](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/long-reference-video-models); [oneneural](https://www.oneneural.ai/blog/best-open-video-generation-models-2026); [builderai.tools](https://builderai.tools/blog/best-open-source-video-generation-stack-2026) (via search summary)

### Inferences
- **Practical 16 GB envelope:** about 81-121 frames (3.4-5 s at 24 fps) per generation at 480p-720p. Longer shots need chaining (last frame becomes the next first frame), a long-video method (FramePack, LongCat continuation, SkyReels), or looping.
- A 5080 is probably somewhat slower than a 4090 for diffusion (less VRAM bandwidth and fewer cores, but native FP4/FP8 Blackwell paths). Planning numbers: **about 2-5 min per 5 s 720p clip with distilled/lightning settings, 20-45 min without.** A 7-minute film made entirely of 5 s generated shots (about 84 clips, plus rejects at 2-4 takes each) would be an overnight-to-multi-day GPU job. B-roll and plates for selected shots is the realistic scope.
- On a Blackwell card, LTX-2.5's NVFP4 distilled build is the most likely "fast" path. No measured RTX 5080 numbers exist, so a local timing run is the first task.
- *(prior knowledge, not re-verified)* Wan 2.2 A14B natively outputs 16 fps, while TI2V-5B outputs 24 fps at 1280x704. Both need frame-rate conversion or retiming for a 24 fps timeline.

### Gaps
- No measured RTX 5080 (or 4080) timings for LTX-2.3/2.5, HunyuanVideo 1.5, Kandinsky 5 or LongCat-Video.
- No first-party VRAM figure for LTX-2.5 at specific resolutions/frame counts on 16 GB. The vendor "16 GB minimum" claim is contradicted by a 47 GB measurement at 2 MP.
- The size and VRAM needs of MAGI-2 and SkyReels V3 were not verified against primary sources.
- The ltx.io/llm-info page could not be fetched (header overflow).

## Q2. Quality, temporal consistency and controllability (camera, first/last frame, depth/pose control, style LoRAs, consistency across shots)

### Takeaway
Controllability is now good enough for a procedural pipeline. LTX-2.3/2.5 IC-LoRA (union depth/canny/pose) and the Wan 2.2 ecosystem (FLF2V, LoRAs, VACE, Fun variants, lightning LoRAs) both allow **structure-locked video-to-video** (motion and layout from a reference, look from the prompt or a LoRA) and **first/last-frame interpolation**. Wan has the deepest LoRA ecosystem. LTX is faster and has audio, but its picture quality trails Wan at matched effort.

### Cited Findings
- LTX-2.5 modes: T2V, I2V, V2V, text-to-audio, audio-to-video. Official ComfyUI templates cover T2V, I2V and **first/last-frame-to-video**, with example workflows for ICLoRA inpainting/outpainting, motion tracking and **union control**. Native multishot ("wide, medium, and close-up shots" in one generation), auto duration from the described action, and native 4K HDR output (DFR pipeline aimed at EXR output) — [ComfyUI Wiki](https://comfyui-wiki.com/en/news/2026-08-11-ltx-2-5-open-weights-release)
- LTX-2.5 I2V conditioning is `--image PATH FRAME_IDX STRENGTH` (frame index plus strength, so conditioning is not restricted to frame 0 in principle). The card mentions a detailing IC-LoRA pixel upscaler and IC-LoRA training — [HF Lightricks/LTX-2.5](https://huggingface.co/Lightricks/LTX-2.5)
- LTX IC-LoRA "enables precise video-to-video control... by conditioning generation on reference signals like depth maps, pose skeletons, or edge detections". In LTX-2.3 a single checkpoint, `LTX-2.3-22b-IC-LoRA-Union-Control`, handles depth, canny and pose, and needs the most VRAM of the options. LTX-2 had separate Pose/Canny checkpoints. "Motion comes from reference video; prompts control visual style only." Run one IC-LoRA group at a time to avoid memory problems. For I2V, align the first frame with the reference video. Depth can come from DepthCrafter **or Blender synthetic depth renders**. Keep the depth range consistent across frames to avoid flicker — [LTX docs: IC-LoRA](https://docs.ltx.video/open-source-model/usage-guides/ic-lo-ra); [LTX blog: how to use IC-LoRA](https://ltx.io/blog/how-to-use-ic-lora-in-ltx-2); [GitHub Lightricks/ComfyUI-LTXVideo](https://github.com/Lightricks/ComfyUI-LTXVideo)
- LTX quality caveats (third-party): picture quality trails Wan 2.2 at matched effort, photorealism is weaker, the LoRA ecosystem is much smaller than Wan's, audio sync is softer at 16 GB, and long-form output requires chaining segments — [AI Video Sensei](https://aivideosensei.com/guides/ltx-2-local-setup)
- Wan 2.2's ecosystem is "the deepest: LoRAs, VACE, Phantom, GGUF quants, lightning LoRAs, and every UI supports it first", with about 5 s per native clip and no audio — [Thunder Compute](https://www.thundercompute.com/blog/best-open-source-ai-video-generation-models) (via search summary)
- Wan 2.2 has native first-last-frame (FLF2V) workflows in ComfyUI — [ComfyUI docs](https://docs.comfy.org/tutorials/video/wan/wan2_2)
- HunyuanVideo 1.5 supports LoRA fine-tuning (`--use_lora`, training code from 5 Dec 2025) — [HF tencent/HunyuanVideo-1.5](https://huggingface.co/tencent/HunyuanVideo-1.5). It is recommended for realistic human faces at 14 GB — [Thunder Compute](https://www.thundercompute.com/blog/best-open-source-ai-video-generation-models) (via search summary)
- Kandinsky 5 Lite is suggested where permissive weights and camera control both matter, though no optimized production server exists for it yet — [builderai.tools](https://builderai.tools/blog/best-open-source-video-generation-stack-2026) (via search summary)
- SkyReels V3 is described as the best open option for consistent subjects across clips (1-4 reference images). LongCat's long mode is first-frame conditioned only — [Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/long-reference-video-models) (via search summary)
- FramePack: motion can drift over long durations, and quality trails hosted commercial services — [stable-diffusion-art](https://stable-diffusion-art.com/framepack/) (via search summary)

### Inferences
- **The best fit for this pipeline is "procedural guide → generated look"**: render the shot procedurally (exact composition, timing, palette), extract depth/canny (or render a depth pass directly from the scene), and run LTX IC-LoRA union control or Wan VACE/Fun-Control to repaint it in a painterly style. Composition and timing stay authored by code, and the model adds texture, light and secondary motion. This plays to the models' strengths and avoids their weakness: inventing layout and readability.
- **I2V from an existing Qwen-Image plate** is the lowest-risk use: animate a still plate (drifting fog, water, embers, light shafts, slow push-in), then composite procedural layers on top. FLF2V between two plates gives transitions.
- Style consistency across many shots is best held by (a) always conditioning on a plate made in the house style (I2V/FLF), (b) a style LoRA (the Wan ecosystem is strongest; LTX supports IC-LoRA training), and (c) the existing post grade, grain and palette checks over everything. Text-only T2V will drift in style between shots.
- *(prior knowledge, not re-verified)* Wan ecosystem control options: VACE (reference/depth/pose/inpaint/outpaint "all-in-one" for 2.1, community-adapted to 2.2), Wan 2.2 Fun Control/Fun Camera/Fun InP variants, and Uni3C/ReCamMaster-style camera control via Kijai's WanVideoWrapper.

### Gaps
- No systematic, independent comparison of style consistency across many shots for any model.
- Native camera-control support in LTX-2.5 and HunyuanVideo 1.5 was not confirmed (the LTX-2.5 card does not mention camera LoRAs).
- How well IC-LoRA/VACE preserve a flat, vector-like, non-photoreal style (as in Initial Condition) was not found. Most examples are photoreal or anime.

## Q3. Determinism (seeds) and reproducible regeneration of one shot

### Takeaway
All of these pipelines take an explicit seed (ComfyUI sampler nodes expose it, and it can be set through the API), so a shot can be regenerated by storing the full workflow JSON plus the seed. No source documents bit-exact determinism across runs, driver versions or offload settings, so the plan should be to "store the output file and treat regeneration as approximate".

### Cited Findings
- In the ComfyUI API pattern, the seed is injected into the exported API-format workflow before POSTing (for example `workflow["3"]["inputs"]["seed"] = 12345`). The API-format JSON is the complete, replayable job description — [eastondev ComfyUI API batch automation (2026-07-24)](https://eastondev.com/blog/en/posts/ai/20260724-comfyui-api-batch-automation/); [Hermes agent ComfyUI skill docs](https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/docs/user-guide/skills/bundled/creative/creative-comfyui) (via search summary)
- LTX-2.5's distilled model uses a fixed 8-step, CFG=1 schedule, which reduces the number of parameters that must be recorded to reproduce a run — [HF Lightricks/LTX-2.5](https://huggingface.co/Lightricks/LTX-2.5)

### Inferences
- Bit-exact repeats are likely only with an identical model file, quantization, attention backend (SageAttention/xformers/SDPA), block-swap and tiling settings, and driver/PyTorch version. Caches (TeaCache, etc.) and some fused kernels are non-deterministic. Store the workflow JSON, seed, model hashes and node versions in the shot's sidecar, and keep the rendered MP4/PNG frames under `build/cache` as the source of truth (matching the memo-cache pattern from Initial Condition).
- For a "notes loop" (change one thing, keep the rest), fix the seed and vary one input (prompt, strength, guide). Generative variation between seeds is large, so best-of-N with the agent judging a stills sheet (the same loop as for Qwen-Image plates) is the realistic workflow.

### Gaps
- No source tested run-to-run determinism of Wan, LTX or Hunyuan in ComfyUI on consumer GPUs.

**Wan 2.1/2.2 (Apache 2.0), Mochi 1 (Apache 2.0), CogVideoX-2B (Apache 2.0), Kandinsky 5 and LongCat-Video (MIT) are commercial-friendly.** LTX-2.x is free for commercial use under US$10M annual revenue. **HunyuanVideo (1.0 and 1.5) explicitly does not apply in the EU, UK or South Korea.** That is a blocker if the author is EU-based, and it probably extends to FramePack, which is built on HunyuanVideo weights.

## Q5. Practical integration: ComfyUI driven from a script/API, CLIs, upscaling and frame interpolation to 1080p/24 fps

### Takeaway
ComfyUI is the integration point for every model above. The agent exports a workflow in API format, injects prompt/seed/image paths, POSTs to `/prompt`, waits on the WebSocket and downloads from `/view`. This is the same pattern as the existing `local-image` Qwen-Image skill. To reach 1080p/24 fps: generate at 480-720p, upscale (LTX latent 2x upscaler, Hunyuan SR, or SeedVR2/ESRGAN-style), then retime with RIFE to the target fps (or resample from 16 fps).

### Cited Findings
- ComfyUI API pattern: export with File → Export Workflow (API) (UI-saved JSON fails validation); POST `{"prompt": workflow, "client_id": id}` to `/prompt`, which returns a `prompt_id` and possibly `node_errors`; open `ws://host:8188/ws?clientId=...` before posting; completion is an "executing" message with `data.node is None` for that prompt_id; `/history/{prompt_id}` gives filenames, and `/view` returns the file; there are also `/queue` and `/interrupt`. The output key varies per video save node ("gifs", "videos", "images"). Run jobs one at a time on a single GPU — [eastondev ComfyUI API automation](https://eastondev.com/blog/en/posts/ai/20260724-comfyui-api-batch-automation/); [glama comfyui-mcp API doc](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/@PurlieuStudios/comfyui-mcp/blob/75642fe9bb5ae9e150ea1fc63862ae8a45c25953/docs/COMFYUI_API.md) (via search summary)
- Non-ComfyUI paths: HunyuanVideo 1.5 ships `generate.py` with flags (`--enable_step_distill`, `--enable_cache --cache_type`, `--video_length`) plus Diffusers support — [HF tencent/HunyuanVideo-1.5](https://huggingface.co/tencent/HunyuanVideo-1.5). LTX-2.5 ships an `ltx-pipelines` CLI (`--image`, `--num-frames`, `--spatial-upscalings`, `--quantization fp8-cast`) and Diffusers examples — [HF Lightricks/LTX-2.5](https://huggingface.co/Lightricks/LTX-2.5)
- LTX upscaling built in: 2x latent spatial (required for the multi-stage pipeline), 2x latent temporal, and a 2x/4x optional temporal upscale in DFR. A typical recipe is a two-stage draft-then-refine pipeline. The "practical consumer ceiling is 720p to 1080p locally, with a separate upscale pass for delivery" — [HF Lightricks/LTX-2.5](https://huggingface.co/Lightricks/LTX-2.5); [AI Video Sensei](https://aivideosensei.com/guides/ltx-2-local-setup)
- HunyuanVideo 1.5 has a super-resolution stage to 1080p — [HF tencent/HunyuanVideo-1.5](https://huggingface.co/tencent/HunyuanVideo-1.5)
- SeedVR2 (diffusion video upscaler) on 16 GB: possible with BlockSwap (`blocks_to_swap` from 16 upward, `preserve_vram: True`), but reports conflict (one says 18 GB+ even for 3B; a 4080 user had mixed results with the smallest FP8). A 5070 Ti 16 GB upscaled 640x504 → 1370x1080 at about 2.0 fps. Temporal consistency needs batch ≥5, and batch must be 4n+1 — [AInVFX SeedVR2 guide](https://www.ainvfx.com/blog/one-step-4k-video-upscaling-and-beyond-for-free-in-comfyui-with-seedvr2/); [weirdwonderfulai](https://weirdwonderfulai.art/resources/seedvr2-upscaler-for-video-and-images/); [docs.comfy.org SeedVR2](https://docs.comfy.org/tutorials/utility/seedvr2.md) (via search summary)
- RIFE in ComfyUI: ComfyUI-Frame-Interpolation's RIFE VFI (rife47/rife49 recommended) uses integer multipliers. Resampling nodes (e.g. WhiteRabbit "RIFE VFI FPS Resample") convert 16 → 25 fps by source/target fps. LightX2V's Wan pipeline has `"algo": "rife", "target_fps"` — [runcomfy RIFE VFI](https://runcomfy.com/comfyui-nodes/ComfyUI-Frame-Interpolation/RIFE-VFI); [runcomfy WhiteRabbit](https://www.runcomfy.com/comfyui-nodes/comfyui-WhiteRabbit/rife-fps-resample); [LightX2V VFI docs](https://lightx2v-en.readthedocs.io/en/latest/method_tutorials/video_frame_interpolation.html)

### Inferences
- Recommended delivery chain for a generated plate: generate 720p (or 480p for drafts) at the model's native fps → upscale to 1080p (LTX latent upscaler or SeedVR2; a cheap ESRGAN-type frame upscaler is often enough for soft atmospheric plates that will be graded, grained and composited) → RIFE to 24 fps (16 → 24 = 1.5x needs a resampling node, or 2x to 32 then drop frames) → hand frames to the existing Python compositor as a plate source (read with OpenCV per frame, like a static plate but indexed by `s.t`).
- Upscale before interpolating, so the expensive upscaler processes fewer frames (search-summary recommendation, consistent with the cost figures above).
- Wrap all of this as a `local-video` skill mirroring `local-image`: a ComfyUI server start/stop, versioned API workflows per mode (i2v, flf2v, v2v-control, t2v), a seed/sidecar JSON, contact-sheet review of 4-6 frames per take, and best-of-N.
- *(prior knowledge, not re-verified)* Kijai's ComfyUI-WanVideoWrapper adds block swap, torch.compile, SageAttention, VACE, FLF and many Wan add-ons. city96's ComfyUI-GGUF loads GGUF-quantized Wan/Hunyuan/LTX transformers. Native ComfyUI nodes cover Wan 2.2, LTX and Hunyuan 1.5 templates.

### Gaps
- No tested end-to-end 16 GB recipe for generate → SeedVR2 → RIFE to 24 fps was found.
- Windows-specific installation pitfalls (SageAttention/Triton wheels for Blackwell, CUDA 12.8+ PyTorch) were not researched in this session.

## Q6. Known failure modes and strengths for this use

### Takeaway
These models are strong at **atmosphere and texture in motion**: fog, smoke, water, fire, particles, foliage, skies, light, slow camera moves, and animating an existing still. They are weak at **anything the story depends on being exact**: legible text, hands and held objects, object permanence, physical causality, precise timing to a word, and holding identity or style over long durations. That is the inverse of the procedural pipeline, which suggests a hybrid.

### Cited Findings
- LTX: lip-sync precision is the first thing lost at lower quality, audio sync is softer at 16 GB, picture quality trails Wan, and photorealism is weaker. The advice is to "regenerate the shot rather than fight it" — [AI Video Sensei](https://aivideosensei.com/guides/ltx-2-local-setup)
- FramePack and long autoregressive methods: motion drifts over long durations — [stable-diffusion-art](https://stable-diffusion-art.com/framepack/) (via search summary)
- Long-form output from LTX (and generally) requires chaining segments — [AI Video Sensei](https://aivideosensei.com/guides/ltx-2-local-setup)
- LTX-2.5's automated artifact score: Pro 0.28, Fast 0.39 (lower is cleaner; vendor-run) — [Runpod](https://www.runpod.io/blog/ltx-2-5-the-open-weights-world-model-built-for-speed-and-how-to-run-it-on-runpod) (via search summary)
- IC-LoRA depth control flickers if the depth range is not kept consistent across frames — [LTX IC-LoRA docs](https://docs.ltx.video/open-source-model/usage-guides/ic-lo-ra) (via search summary)

### Inferences
- The well-known failure modes of diffusion video (morphing and melting objects, extra or merged fingers, garbled text, objects appearing or disappearing, "floaty" non-physical motion, flicker in fine detail, colour and contrast drift across chained segments) map directly onto the author's checklist in LESSONS §5: proportion, physical logic (the falling knife), readability and leftovers. Shots where those matter should stay procedural.
- Good uses for Initial Condition-style films: animated background plates (an I2V of each Qwen-Image plate with "slow drift, volumetric haze, embers"), atmospheric b-roll between narrated beats, FLF transitions between two plates, seamless loops (FLF with first frame = last frame), and V2V restyling of procedural renders for texture. Generated content should be graded through the existing palette guards, since models will introduce forbidden hues.
- Hold durations: a 5 s generation will not fill a 10-20 s hold. Use loops, slow-motion/RIFE stretching (with artifacts), chaining, or a procedural parallax camera over a generated plate.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study of failure rates (e.g. percentage of usable takes) for stylized, non-photoreal animation.

## Q7. Coexistence with other local servers on one 16 GB card

### Takeaway
They do not coexist. Every 16 GB recipe above already uses the full card plus offloading into system RAM, so the video stack must run alone: stop the Qwen-Image/ComfyUI image server, TTS and SFX servers first. System RAM (64 GB recommended) becomes the second bottleneck.

### Cited Findings
- Wan 2.2 14B FP8 at 720p needs about 14-16 GB even with T5 offloaded — [willitrunai](https://willitrunai.com/blog/wan-2-2-vram-requirements) (via search summary)
- HunyuanVideo 1.5's 14 GB minimum already assumes model offloading — [HF tencent/HunyuanVideo-1.5](https://huggingface.co/tencent/HunyuanVideo-1.5)
- NVIDIA pairs 16 GB of VRAM with 64 GB of system RAM for LTX-2.3. LTX-2.5's text encoder alone is a 12B Gemma 4 (bf16 or int8) — [Thunder Compute](https://www.thundercompute.com/blog/best-open-source-ai-video-generation-models) (via search summary); [ComfyUI Wiki](https://comfyui-wiki.com/en/news/2026-08-11-ltx-2-5-open-weights-release)
- FramePack's real limit on small cards was system RAM (>20 GB resident) — [lilting.ch](https://lilting.ch/articles/local-long-video-fastwan-framepack-rocm) (via search summary)
- LTX: run one IC-LoRA group at a time to avoid memory problems — [LTX blog](https://ltx.io/blog/how-to-use-ic-lora-in-ltx-2)
- SeedVR2 on 16 GB needs block swap, so it also cannot share the card — [AInVFX](https://www.ainvfx.com/blog/one-step-4k-video-upscaling-and-beyond-for-free-in-comfyui-with-seedvr2/)

### Inferences
- The project already learned "one GPU stack at a time" (LESSONS §3.6 and §6). Video generation extends this rule: schedule video jobs as batch phases (for example, overnight "generate all plates for act 2") behind the same DONE-marker job chaining used for renders, and never alongside the 15 GB image model. If ComfyUI is shared between Qwen-Image and video workflows in one server, call its `/free` (unload models) endpoint between phases *(prior knowledge, not re-verified)*.
- Upscaling (SeedVR2) and generation should also be separate phases. RIFE is light and could run alongside the CPU-bound Python compositor.

### Gaps
- No measurement of ComfyUI's model-swap overhead (loading 14-22B weights from disk per phase) on this machine.
- Footnote, cloud only (not researched in depth): LTX offers a managed API (1080p, 23.7 s for a 10 s clip per vendor), and Wan 2.5-3.0 are API-only — [Runpod](https://www.runpod.io/blog/ltx-2-5-the-open-weights-world-model-built-for-speed-and-how-to-run-it-on-runpod); [Atlas Cloud](https://www.atlascloud.ai/blog/tips/is-wan-3.0-open-source).
